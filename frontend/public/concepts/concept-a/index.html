Scientific Abstract

We present the architecture and audited results of GWLab, an interactive research laboratory dedicated to the verification of gravitational-wave matched filtering and Bayesian parameter estimation on compact binary coalescences. Operating on a frozen 4.0-second observation segment under LIGO Hanford (H1) design sensitivity, GWLab isolates the stationary-phase approximation (TaylorF2 3.5PN) across an active band of 20–512 Hz. We present authoritative benchmark evidence for the Run A Anchor result ($\ln Z = 201646.1413 \pm 0.2374\,\text{nats}$), document the historical independent-seed repeatability discrepancy ($\Delta \ln Z = 2.9720\,\text{nats}$, ratio $8.92\sigma$), and provide deterministic exploration of the binary parameter manifold.

1. Foundations of Frequency-Domain Gravitational Waves

For non-spinning, circular binary inspirals, the gravitational strain in the frequency domain is evaluated using the stationary-phase approximation (SPA). Under this formalism, the Fourier transform $\tilde{h}(f)$ decouples cleanly into an analytical amplitude prefactor $\mathcal{A}(f) \propto f^{-7/6}$ and a post-Newtonian Fourier phase trajectory $\Psi(f)$ containing conservative and dissipative relativistic corrections up to order 3.5PN:

$$\tilde{h}(f) = \mathcal{A}(f)\,\exp\left[-i\,\Psi(f)\right], \quad \mathcal{A}(f) = \sqrt{\frac{5}{24}}\,\pi^{-2/3}\,\left(\frac{G\,\mathcal{M}_c}{c^3}\right)^{5/6}\,\frac{c}{d_L}\,f^{-7/6}$$
